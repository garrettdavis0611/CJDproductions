06

Eleven things to settle before you order

worst consequence first
01 · blocker

Measure the ceiling in four places.

The whole set is drawn to 92⅞″ frames under a 96″ ceiling. At 94″ or 95″ you drop to the 79¼″ frames — and then the 88″ shelf, the 83″ rail and the 64″ long hang all disappear. That is a different closet, not an adjustment. Nothing else on this page is worth doing until this number is confirmed.

02 · clash

Your crown molding does not fit.

A 92⅞″ frame under a 96″ ceiling leaves 3⅛″. Standard crown runs 3½–4½″ — it will not land above the frames. Three ways out: a 3″ profile, a flat filler panel with a small cap, or crown on the door wall only and a clean scribed filler everywhere else. Decide this before you buy stock, because it also changes the 32 lf.

03 · clash

Only 1⅜″ between the frames and the door opening.

A 29½″ opening centred on a 78″ wall starts 24¼″ from each corner, and the runs come out 22⅞″. Your instinct was right: the closet-side casing comes off entirely, and even then you have a hair over an inch of reveal. Check the jamb is plumb while you are in there — you cannot scribe your way out of a negative number.

04 · clash

L2 and R2 put opposing drawer banks in a 32¼″ aisle.

Both drawer bays sit at the same depth into the room, facing each other. A full-extension KOMPLEMENT drawer comes out around 20″, leaving roughly 12″ to squeeze past — and nothing at all if two people open drawers at once, which is the whole point of a shared closet. Stagger the banks by moving L2's drawers to L1, or take R2 to double hang.

05 · fix

The tower's 42″ shelf strands 2″.

Five drawers fill the bottom 40″, then the first shoe shelf sits at 42″. That 2″ between them holds nothing. Either drop the shelf to sit right on the drawer stack, or go to four drawers and start the shoes lower — you gain a third shoe row either way.

06 · verify

Four drawers will not evenly fill 45″.

That is an 11¼″ pitch, and KOMPLEMENT drawer heights snap to the frame's hole spacing rather than to whatever number you want. Four medium drawers land closer to 38″, leaving 7″ loose under the rail. Let the planner set the stack, then read the real rail height back off it — the same applies to the tower's five-in-40″.

07 · verify

Four rod lights, six rails.

As drawn there are six rails and four rod lights, so two of them go unlit — almost certainly the lower rails in L1 and R1, which are the ones you reach into every morning. Either buy six, or decide on purpose that the lower rails run dark and let the ceiling fixture cover them.

08 · verify

Two of your three article numbers are not in this plan.

705.881.67 is right, and you need four of it. But 405.881.59 is a 19⅝″ frame that appears nowhere here, and 805.881.62 is 29½ × 22⅞ × 79⅛ — wrong depth and wrong height for the tower, which needs 29½ × 13¾ × 92⅞. Take the tower number and every KOMPLEMENT number straight off the planner's own list.

09 · note

The bench does not go in the middle.

A 16 × 32″ bench set in the aisle leaves 16¼″ to walk past it. But the plan hands you two open corners behind the runs, 22⅞ × 14¼″ each, currently doing nothing. One takes the bench, the other takes a hamper — and neither costs you a bay.

10 · note

Two numbers in the sheet do not match the frames.

Section 2 lists the frames at 93⅛″ tall; the article you confirmed is 92⅞″. And 39⅜ × 2 = 78¾, not 78⅝ — so the leftover wall is 14¼″, the tower gap is 32¼″, and the fillers are 1⅜″ a side. Everything on these drawings uses the corrected figures. Also worth confirming: these are wall-mounted frames, so if they hang clear of the floor, every height on this page shifts by that gap.

11 · checks out

Flooring, hanging and pull counts all reconcile.

78 × 93 is 50.4 sq ft, so your ~50 plus 10% is right — buy 56. The plan yields 19′ 8¼″ of hanging across six rails, and 11 drawers against 11 brass pulls. Your 80 hangers fill a little under half the rail, which is the right place to start.

07

Order of work

the sequence is load-bearing
  1. Strip and patch.Wire shelving, brackets, anchors out. Patch, sand. Then measure the ceiling and mark every stud on all three walls — check 01 before anything is bought.
  2. Reverse the door swing.Carpenter. Pull the closet-side casing while the door is off; check 03 says it is not going back on.
  3. Flooring.Wall to wall, before cabinetry. Nothing sits on top of a floor you still have to slide in.
  4. Electrical rough-in.Rod-light drops in four bays, tower strip feed, ceiling box relocated to the aisle centreline, dimmer. The room is never this empty again.
  5. Paint.Walls, ceiling and trim in the one evergreen. Cut in behind where the runs land — you will see slivers of it forever.
  6. Set the two runs.L1 first, hard against the door wall, anchored into studs. Work back. Confirm the run stops at 78¾″.
  7. Scribe the tower between them.Set it centred, then fit the two 1⅜″ fillers to the actual gap you measure — not the one on this drawing.
  8. Trim, caulk, touch up.Base on the door wall only. Crown by whichever of the three answers you picked in check 02.
  9. Lighting terminations and hardware.Rods, strip, fixture, dimmer. Then pulls, hooks, mirror, bench, hangers.
